# Example: a useful profile conversation

All details are invented. The objective is an operations role; there is enough confirmed information to start a search.

The audit finds a useful coordination example, an unclear management claim, an unsupported percentage and conflicting start months. It does not ask the candidate to rewrite their whole history.

**Assistant:** “You have a useful example of improving the booking process. I can't yet tell what you changed personally or what improved afterwards. Walk me through one booking before and after the checklist.”

**Candidate:** “Previously people sent requests to whichever volunteer they knew. I made one shared sheet, checked it each morning and added a checklist for room setup. I don't have a count of errors.”

**Assistant:** “That supports organising requests and standardising handovers. We'll leave out the 30% claim. Did you create the sheet and checklist yourself, or adapt something the team already used?”

**Candidate:** “I made the checklist. A colleague made the first sheet; I maintained it.”

The assistant stores the distinction with this conversation as provenance, then proposes: “Created a room-setup checklist and maintained a shared bookings sheet to make requests and handovers easier to track.” This is a **draft**, not yet approved reusable wording.

Next questions, in order:

1. “When you helped the two volunteers, what decisions were yours and who supervised them?” Clarifies scope; avoids inventing management experience.
2. “Your notes give January and March as the start month. Which is right, or should we keep just 2023 for now?” Resolves the date conflict without blocking useful work.
3. “Which parts of the role would you like more of in your next job?” Improves search direction rather than merely polishing the CV.

The story is saved in `profile/stories.md` straight away:

```md
## Booking checklist (rank B)
- Role and dates: Operations Coordinator, Cedar Workshop Collective, 2023–2026
- Situation: requests went to whichever volunteer people knew, so bookings were missed at handover
- Task: keep weekly workshop bookings and room setup on track
- Action: maintained the shared bookings sheet (a colleague made the first version); created a room-setup checklist; checked the sheet each morning
- Result: colleagues could see outstanding requests and take over shifts; no count of errors before or after
- Learned: (not asked yet)
- Good for: process improvement, organisation, "tell me about a time you improved something"
- Source: told in chat 2026-10-01 (fictional)
```

It's a B, not an A, because the result has no number. One more question could move it up: "Roughly how many bookings a week went through the sheet?" An honest estimate, labelled as one, is fine.

After each answer, update only the relevant files. At the end, show what was confirmed, what wording remains a proposal, what is still unknown and the next useful question. If the candidate stops, save that point for the next conversation.
