# Scoring jobs

Be strict. Most jobs should score 2 or 3. Recommend only 4s and 5s, unless the person asks for more. Fit beats volume: in Nox, applications below a strong fit didn't lead to interviews, and weak applications cost the person time at the review stage.

Apply the same standard to every job, every search. Re-read this page and the calibration notes in `profile/preferences.md` before scoring.

## 1. Dealbreakers first

Check the dealbreakers in `profile/preferences.md`, plus these, which are always yes/no:

- the location, or for remote jobs the allowed countries, doesn't work for them;
- they'd need sponsorship and the job can't offer it (from `profile/answers.md`);
- the posted salary is clearly below their floor;
- it requires a clearance, licence, certification or language they don't have;
- it requires far more experience than they have (for example 10+ years when they have 3).

Any one rules the job out, however appealing it is. **Unknown isn't a dealbreaker:** if the ad doesn't mention salary or sponsorship, note it as unknown.

## 2. Reasoning before the score

Write the evidence first, briefly, then score. Never the other way round.

- **Can they do it?** Take the job's top five requirements. For each, name direct evidence from the profile, transferable evidence, or a gap.
- **Do they want it?** Compare with the work they enjoy, their level, sectors and growth goals.
- **Practical:** location and office days, salary, hours, and anything unknown.

## 3. The score

| Score | Meaning | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | Meets nearly every requirement with direct evidence, and it's exactly what they want. Rare. | Recommend first |
| 4 | Meets the core requirements, with one or two learnable gaps, and it appeals. | Recommend |
| 3 | Real gaps, or doubts about whether they'd want it. | Mention only if the batch is short, and say why |
| 2 | Missing core requirements, or not what they want. | Leave out |
| 1 | Wrong field or level. | Leave out |

Record it in the tracker's `fit` field as score, reason and main gap, for example `4/5: runs a similar fleet; gap: no SAP`.

## Calibrate

On the first search, show five scored jobs and ask what they think of each, and why. Save their reactions in `profile/preferences.md` → Calibration, and adjust. If they keep choosing jobs you scored low (or rejecting ones you scored high), update the preferences, not just the scores.

A score isn't the chance of getting hired, and it never authorises an application. Never factor in age, gender, ethnicity or any other protected characteristic.
