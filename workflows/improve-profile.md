# Improve my profile

Goal: better context, so better jobs and better applications. Default to a short, high-value review (10–15 minutes), not a questionnaire. Offer a deeper pass if they want one.

## 1. Read and diagnose

Read everything in `profile/`, plus any new files in `inbox/` and the target job if they've given one. An old polished CV isn't automatically right, and something missing from it doesn't mean they lack the experience.

Look for, in order of value:

- **Conflicts:** dates, titles, locations or qualifications that disagree between sources.
- **Weak stories:** fewer than 8 in `stories.md`, or too many ranked B or C. A story is weak when the problem, what *they* did, or the result is unclear.
- **Unanswered standard questions** in `answers.md`: right to work, the four salary facts, notice period, start date.
- **Fuzzy targets:** roles, locations or dealbreakers too vague to score jobs strictly.
- **No real voice:** `voice.md` has no samples of their own writing, or only AI-written ones.
- **Claims that outrun the evidence**, including team results presented as personal ones.

Tell them the top three findings in a few lines. No "profile completeness" score, and no judgement of gaps or unusual careers.

## 2. Ask

Save the plan in `profile/open-questions.md` so it survives a new chat. Ask one question at a time (or small groups if they prefer), and say why it matters. Ask neutral questions, never ones that hint at an impressive answer:

- "Your CV says 2021–2023 and your LinkedIn says 2022–2023. Which is right?"
- "What part did you own, and what did the team handle?"
- "What changed because of it? A rough number is fine if you say it's an estimate."
- "What did people rely on you for in an ordinary week?"
- "Which parts of that job would you want more of?"

Follow up on good answers rather than marching through a list. Turn B stories into A stories with one or two specific probes. Don't demand metrics that don't exist: a clear qualitative result is fine. Don't ask about health, family or identity to fill space.

If they're unsure what they want, suggest two or three directions and compare a few real job ads for each. When preferences conflict, talk through the trade-off instead of quietly turning a "nice to have" into a dealbreaker.

## 3. Save

Write each answer into the file that owns it, straight away:

- facts and history → `facts.md`, `experience.md`, `claims.json` (with source and date);
- examples → `stories.md`, in its STAR format with a rank;
- standard and reusable answers → `answers.md`;
- targets and dealbreakers → `preferences.md`;
- writing samples and words to avoid → `voice.md`.

Only confirmed facts go into applications. Mark anything you inferred as a draft and show significant rewording for their OK. Keep old evidence when correcting it, and never change what was already submitted.

If they want a general CV refreshed, follow [documents](../docs/documents.md) and [writing](../docs/writing.md).

## 4. Wrap up

Summarise what improved and what's still open, and save the next question in `open-questions.md`. Suggest another pass when something changes (a new job, new feedback, a new direction), but don't turn every task into a profile review or keep asking a question they've skipped.
