# Prepare for an interview

Confirm employer, role, round, date/timezone, format and any stated assessment rules from the user's account or authorised relevant correspondence. An invitation to propose times is not a booked meeting. Email access is optional; the user can provide the relevant invitation text. Do not send replies or accept calendar invitations without instruction.

Read the exact submitted CV and answers, commitments, receipt and current confirmed profile. If current evidence differs from earlier submitted wording, help the candidate address the discrepancy honestly. Preserve submitted history.

Research the company, product, customers, business model, current priorities and the actual role using current primary sources. Cite sources and dates. Separate employer claims, corroborated facts and hypotheses. Research named interviewers through relevant professional material; verify identity and avoid sensitive/private profiling. Do not claim a guessed hiring manager is confirmed.

Create `interview/briefing.md` in the existing application package: a concise pre-call summary followed by useful company/role context, logistics, requirements-to-evidence mapping and questions the candidate should ask. Include uncertainties rather than guessing revenue, runway, culture or interview format.

Create `interview/practice.md`: roughly 8–12 prioritised questions for this round, draft spoken answers, short recall anchors, supporting claim/story IDs and plausible follow-ups. Keep answers natural; use STAR when helpful, not mechanically. Cover motivation, relevant achievements, trade-offs, collaboration, learning and actual gaps. Don't invent a commitment to relocate, close a business or accept a salary.

For rehearsal, ask one question and wait. Let the person attempt it before revealing a model answer. Give specific feedback grounded in their evidence. Adapt difficulty and timing to preference. Fictional cases are clearly labelled practice; they are not leaked or predicted employer questions. Respect assessment rules and help the person develop independent understanding.

After a debrief, record actual questions and feedback separately from predictions. Promote newly confirmed facts/stories into the profile, with date and scope. Update the tracker only for evidenced milestones. Preserve earlier-round notes before substantial revisions. Do not turn rejection into proof of a particular personal weakness.
